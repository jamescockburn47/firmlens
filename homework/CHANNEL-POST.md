# Follow-on message for the residency channel

Here are the homework details. There are two ways to take part; feedback is just as welcome as code.

**First, use the site yourself.** Try to answer a question you actually care about: which firms use a particular tool, whether an announcement describes a trial or a rollout, or what has changed since an earlier check. Notice where the site helps and where it gets in your way.

**1. Give feedback — around 15–20 minutes**

Review any of these:

- **Accuracy:** open a summary and its original source. Does the source support the important claims, dates and figures? Does the summary miss a qualification? A genuine quotation does not prove the whole summary is right.
- **Breadth:** which firms, jurisdictions or kinds of AI use should be included? Please link to a real source for any suggested addition.
- **Functionality:** do the search, filters, source links and history help answer your question? What should the app do that it currently does not?
- **Design:** what is clear, confusing, awkward or enjoyable? Try it on your phone if you can. Wording, layout, colour, accessibility and ease of use all count.

Post here using this simple format:

> I was trying to…
> I noticed…
> I would keep/change… because…
> Link or screenshot:

You do not need to find a fault. Tell us what works well too. If you cannot open a source, say so rather than assuming the summary is correct or incorrect.

**2. Make one small improvement — optional**

Start with something you noticed yourself, then:

1. Create a free account at https://github.com/signup and verify your email.
2. If you would like contributor access, send me your GitHub username and one sentence about the change you have in mind. I will invite you; accept the invitation in GitHub. You can also use a fork without an invitation.
3. Follow the guide below to open a local copy with Codex or Claude Code. It includes copy-and-paste setup prompts and a preview with invented data. You do not need a Vercel account, a Neon account or my credentials.
4. Ask your agent to investigate your observation. Decide what you want changed, then have it implement that one change on your branch. Avoid a general “audit this codebase” exercise.
5. Open the local result and repeat your original task. Check that you prefer it and that it works. For AI-related changes, check the evidence or a labelled example as well as the code. Ask the agent to run the project's checks and explain anything it could not test.
6. Submit a pull request — a proposed change for me to assess — and post its link here. Include your original observation, why you chose the change, before/after evidence and the checks you actually ran. Do not merge or deploy it yourself.

A clearer label, a better mobile interaction or an honest explanation of missing evidence can be a strong contribution. The point is to decide what is worth changing and whether the result is better, not to produce the most code. If setup gets in the way, use the feedback route.

**What I will look for:** your own observation; a clear reason for the change; care with sources and AI claims; a focused proposal; and evidence that you checked the result yourself. This follows our discussion of judgment and taste: the agent can suggest and build, but you decide what is useful, credible and well designed.

We will publicly acknowledge contributors, including useful feedback. Please tell me how you would like to be credited.
