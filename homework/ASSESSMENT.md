# Questions to guide your review

These are prompts, not a checklist you must complete. Choose the ones relevant to what you noticed.

| Area | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Usefulness | What question did I bring? Did the app help answer it? What would make me return? |
| Design and taste | What is easy or awkward to read? What would I make more prominent? Why do I prefer my alternative? |
| Functionality | Did a search, filter or link behave as I expected? Can I explain the steps to someone else? |
| Accessibility | Can I complete my task on a phone, with the keyboard or at increased zoom? Is important meaning conveyed only by colour? |
| AI output | Does each important claim follow from the source? Are a plan, a pilot and a rollout kept distinct? Is the firm's own claim presented as independently proven? |
| Uncertainty | Can I distinguish “the source says this”, “the source contradicts this” and “I cannot establish this”? What did I not test? |

## How James will assess an optional PR

1. **Observation:** what did the resident actually encounter on the site?
2. **Judgment:** why was this worth changing? Was a trade-off or agent suggestion questioned?
3. **Scope:** does the PR address one understandable problem or preference?
4. **Result:** can James see the before/after difference and repeat the check?
5. **Honesty:** are test results, source support and remaining limits described accurately?

A preference need not be dressed up as a bug. “I find this clearer because…” is a valid design argument. For a factual AI-output criticism, provide the source passage. A valid quotation is not proof that every sentence in a summary is supported.

James may accept the change, ask for a revision or decide it does not suit the app. A thoughtful proposal can be valuable even if it is not merged. Route 1 feedback is a complete contribution in its own right.
